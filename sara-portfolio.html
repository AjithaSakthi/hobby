✦ Medium 01

Canvas
Painting

Canvas painting is the marriage of imagination and texture — where each stroke breathes life onto woven fabric. From Renaissance masters to modern abstractionists, the canvas has been the stage for humanity's most vivid expressions.

📜 A Brief History

Canvas replaced wooden panels in the 15th–16th centuries, first embraced in Venice for its lightweight portability. By the 17th century, oil on canvas became the dominant medium for European masters. Today it spans everything from intimate studies to vast installations.

🖌️ Tip: Priming

Always prime with gesso — 2 to 3 coats, sanding lightly between layers. This prevents paint from soaking into the fibres and gives you a luminous, archival surface that holds colour beautifully for decades.

🎨 Technique: Glazing

Build depth by applying thin, transparent layers of paint (glazes) over a dried base. Each glaze shifts the colour temperature subtly, creating a jewel-like luminosity impossible to achieve with opaque paint alone.

✨ Technique: Impasto

Load your brush or palette knife heavily and apply paint in thick, textured strokes. Van Gogh's iconic swirls are impasto in full glory — the raised texture catches light dynamically and adds tactile energy to a painting.

✦ Medium 02

Oil
Pastel

Oil pastels are the most tactile of all drawing media — waxy, creamy sticks that glide with buttery richness. Uniquely immediate, they need no drying time and invite bold blending with fingertips, letting colour sing right from the first mark.

📜 A Brief History

Oil pastels were developed by Sennelier in Paris in the 1940s at the request of Pablo Picasso and Henri Goetz. Unlike chalk pastels, their oil-and-wax binder makes them permanent, vibrant, and endlessly blendable — a favourite of Picasso throughout his late career.

🖌️ Tip: Blending

Use your fingertip, a tortillon, or even mineral spirits on a brush to blend oil pastels seamlessly. Work light colours into dark for highlights, and build up layers gradually — the medium rewards patient layering with extraordinary depth.

🎨 Technique: Sgraffito

Apply multiple thick layers of oil pastel, then scratch through with a toothpick or palette knife to reveal the colours beneath. This creates stunning textural effects — great for foliage, fur, or intricate patterns.

✨ Paper Choice

Use textured paper with good tooth — pastel paper, watercolour paper, or canvas board. The texture grabs the pigment and allows you to build up rich, luminous layers without the surface becoming saturated too quickly.

✦ Medium 03

Pencil
Sketch

The pencil is the most primal artistic tool — nothing between the hand and the thought. A single graphite line can whisper or shout, suggest or define. Pencil sketching is thinking made visible, the language every visual idea speaks first.

📜 A Brief History

Graphite was discovered in Borrowdale, England in the 1560s. Early artists wrapped raw graphite in string or wood. By the 18th century, Conté and Hardtmuth refined the modern pencil. Today, pencils range from 9H (hardest, lightest) to 9B (softest, darkest).

🖌️ Tip: Pencil Grades

Use H pencils (2H, 4H) for initial light construction lines and fine detail. Switch to B pencils (2B, 4B, 6B) for rich darks and expressive shading. A 2B is the ideal everyday pencil — versatile enough for both line and tone.

🎨 Technique: Cross-Hatching

Build tone with overlapping sets of parallel lines at different angles. The more layers, the darker the value. Vary the spacing and pressure to control gradients subtly — cross-hatching gives drawings a classic, timeless quality.

✨ Technique: Blending

Use a tortillon, cotton swab, or your fingertip to smudge graphite into smooth gradients. Then re-sharpen edges with a kneaded eraser — pull it to a fine point to lift out highlights with precision and restore white.
